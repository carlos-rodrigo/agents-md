# Visual Direction

Visual design organizes attention and gives the product a recognizable voice. Novelty is neither required nor sufficient.

## Derive, do not decorate

Base choices on:

- the surface mode and task;
- existing brand/product authority;
- subject-specific materials and content;
- reading density and environmental use;
- accessibility, localization, and performance constraints.

Avoid aesthetic roulette (“pick an extreme”), category costumes, and anti-pattern inversion. A common font, grid, or component is correct when it fits the product and performs well.

## Typography

- Choose type for legibility, language coverage, loading cost, and brand fit.
- Use a clear hierarchy and comfortable measure; test real content and wrapping.
- System and established product fonts are valid defaults.
- A display face is useful only when the mode and brand benefit; do not force a pairing.
- Use numerals, code, and data treatments that support comparison and scanning.

## Color

- Start with semantic roles: page, surface, text ranks, border, focus, action, information, success, warning, danger.
- Verify contrast and forced/high-contrast modes where supported.
- Pair color with text, icon, shape, or pattern.
- Let saturation and palette breadth follow the mode and use scene, not a universal formula.

## Composition

- Structure must encode content: order, grouping, ownership, comparison, or hierarchy.
- Use cards only when a bounded object or action needs a container.
- Prefer intrinsic grid/flex behavior and stable reading order.
- Asymmetry, overlap, or unusual topology is valid when it clarifies the concept and survives responsiveness—not as proof of creativity.
- Keep primary action and current state findable within seconds.

## Imagery and detail

Use real or honestly illustrative imagery that carries product meaning. Texture, depth, illustration, and atmospheric effects are optional materials; remove any detail that cannot explain its relationship to the subject, hierarchy, or interaction.

## Specificity test

Ask:

- Could this exact visual direction be pasted onto a competitor?
- Does the memorable element demonstrate a product mechanism or merely create a mood?
- Is the result recognizably part of the existing product?
- Would removing a detail reduce meaning, hierarchy, or useful character?

Revise generic or unsupported choices without forcing a theatrical replacement.
